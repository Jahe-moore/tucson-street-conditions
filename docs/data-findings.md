## Findings and what to do

| # | Finding | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2,782 neighborhood segments were last scored in 2020, before most Prop 411 neighborhood work (minor-street treatments: 152 in 2022, 666 in 2024, 823 in 2026) | Worst-first ranks by score. A street scored in 2020 has had six more years of wear, so its score likely overstates its condition and it may rank lower on the list than it should | Re-inspect the 2020 segments before using them in a worst-first list, starting in the wards with the most of them |
| 2 | Of 67 segments repaired after their last inspection, none still shows a poor score | Good sign: the City re-scores repaired streets, so recent repairs aren't polluting the ranking | No action. Keep the check in the report |
| 3 | Wards 3, 5 and 6 have the most pavement in poor or worse shape (about 39%); Ward 1 the least (31.7%) | Gives each council office a fair comparison, because it's weighted by area, not segment count | Show ward results side by side for the oversight commission |
| 4 | Ward 3 has the most segments below 25 (43) despite the fewest miles | Small ward, concentrated worst streets | Check Ward 3's worst-first list against the current project schedule |
| 5 | 25 ward-field problems: 19 county districts ("BOS n") and 6 City roads with a blank ward | Blank-ward City roads are invisible to every ward office | Fix the 6 blank wards at the source (City GIS) |
| 6 | 141 segments have no score; 119 have no inspection date | They drop out of averages and rankings silently in a spreadsheet | Ask TDOT whether these are unrated or a data gap |
| 7 | 302 major segments are under a pavement-cut moratorium, ending between 2027-02-17 and 2031-09-28 | Crews can check these before planning a cut | Give the permit desk the dig-check view |

## Limits

- One snapshot. Trend over time starts with this load; the City layer keeps only the latest score.
- Band names follow the ASTM D6433 scale. Whether the City uses the same cut-offs is assumed, to confirm with staff.
- No cost figures. No published per-mile unit cost was found, so the backlog isn't priced.
- The moratorium layer covers major streets only. Local streets have a separate 36-month rule in the fee schedule that this data can't check.
- Some roads in the layer are County, ADOT, private or university roads. Ward views include only wards 1 to 6.
